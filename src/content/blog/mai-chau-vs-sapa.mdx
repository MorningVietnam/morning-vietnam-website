## The short answer

Sapa if you want altitude, Fansipan, and the famous photographs. Mai Châu 
if you want to live in a valley with a Thai family for two days.

Both are 4-5 hours from Hanoi. Both have rice terraces. But almost nothing
else about them is the same.

## Geography

**Sapa** sits at 1,500m in the Hoàng Liên Son range, Vietnam's highest 
mountains. You wake up above the clouds, hike up to Fansipan (3,143m — 
either on foot or by cable car), and look down on a patchwork of villages.

**Mai Châu** is a valley floor at 220m. You wake up with the valley around 
you, cycle to the next village over, and have lunch at a stilt house. The 
mountains are the horizon, not the destination.

## Who shows up where

Sapa attracts travelers who want the signature shot — misty terraces, red 
headscarves, cable car selfies. It's beautiful and often crowded.

Mai Châu attracts travelers who've been through Sapa before, or who skipped 
it on purpose. Our Mai Châu guests tend to be couples, solo travelers, 
and small friend groups who want a slower, more hands-on trip.

## Season matters

Both valleys have four distinct windows:

- **Jan–Feb** — cool, dry, Tết atmosphere, Sapa may have mist/rain
- **Mar–Apr** — green season starts, both excellent
- **May–Sep** — monsoon, both runnable with rain-friendly alternatives, 
  Mai Châu's lowland rice is at its most vivid
- **Oct–Dec** — golden harvest in Sapa (famous), autumn clarity in Mai Châu

## Which tour we'd suggest

If this is your first time in North Vietnam and you want views: **Sapa**.

If you've done the hits and want depth: **Mai Châu**.
